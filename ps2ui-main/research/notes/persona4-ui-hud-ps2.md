# Persona 4 — PS2 UI / HUD / Menu Aesthetics

## Title Screen

- **Color**: Warm golden yellow (`#F5D000` range) is the dominant title card color — a deliberate inversion of Persona 3's midnight blue. The Persona 4 title screen radiates optimism: bright yellow background with black bold sans-serif type. A small golden inami-bear mascot and the Investigation Team silhouette complete the composition.
- **Type treatment**: The logo uses a bold, slightly geometric serif set on the signature gold ground — community IDs suggest **Baskerville** or a Baskerville-adjacent font for the "PERSONA" wordmark. The "4" numeral is large, stylized, rendered in black against yellow.
- **Motion**: More upbeat and energetic than P3's moody title. Animated transitions use quick cuts and bright pops — matching the game's themes of truth, media, and the vivid world behind the TV.

## Main Menu / Social / World Map Screens

- **Layout**: Persona 4 maintains the graphic-design philosophy established in P3 but shifts the entire register from "blue melancholy" to "yellow optimism." Calendar, map, and menu screens all use high-saturation golden yellow as primary backgrounds or strong accents.
- **Panel style**: Black panels with yellow and gold accent lines. The "TV static" motif appears in transitions — horizontal scan lines and CRT-style interlace effects, directly referencing the game's narrative theme of television and media.
- **Multicolor stripes**: A distinctive element unique to P4. Thin horizontal stripes in multiple colors (representing the TV as the game's central metaphor) appear as decorative bands across menus and loading screens. Black is used alongside these stripes "to not be overshadowed by the flashy colors" (Persona 5 dev panel retrospective).
- **Font (PS2 original)**: Like P3, built on system fonts — **Arial** / **Verdana** bold. The dialogue font was identified as **Skip** (Fontworks). The logo uses a serif closer to Baskerville. Menus: "remarkably consistent" bold sans-serif through nearly all text, with "round menus, rounded corners on boxes in stat screens and modal dialogue boxes" (gamefontlibrary analysis).
- **Rounded shapes**: Where P3 used angular, cutting shapes, P4 introduces more rounded corners and softer geometric containers — reflecting the more optimistic, community-focused narrative vs. P3's hard-edged mortality themes.

## Battle UI

- **Core concept**: Battle screens "look like an old CRT TV" — this is intentional, integrating the game's media critique into its visual grammar. Horizontal scan line overlays, slight color bleed, and the general warm-yellow atmosphere persist into combat.
- **Command menu**: Retains the radial/rotating approach from P3 but with a warmer visual treatment. Yellow and black dominant — white text on yellow strips or black panels with yellow borders.
- **Party HP**: Compact character cards at bottom with horizontal HP/SP bars. HP bar fill in a warm amber or green; SP bar in blue. Character portraits are small pixel-art style thumbnails.
- **Status effects**: Icon-based, shown adjacent to character cards. Clear, iconographic, small.
- **Shadow enemy indicators**: Enemy names in bold white or yellow sans-serif at top of screen. No mini-map (dungeon is procedurally generated, minimizes persistent navigation UI).

## Font Character

- **Primary**: Arial / Verdana bold — same system font base as P3. The visual identity comes from bold weight, yellow-black high contrast, and rounded container shapes.
- **Logo**: Baskerville-adjacent serif for "PERSONA" wordmark; large black numeral "4" in geometric style.
- **Dialogue**: Skip (Fontworks) — a warmly proportioned rounded sans, more casual than the menu fonts.
- **Tracking**: Moderate (slightly tighter than P3's wide-tracked menus). The yellow-dominant palette creates perceived density — tighter tracking still reads as open.
- **Rounded corners**: A deliberate softening of P3's angular approach — rounded `border-radius` throughout containers.

## Design Signature

Persona 4 is the PS2 proof that **game UI can carry narrative theme as primary visual language**. The yellow = sunlight = truth equation is literal: the game's mystery is about seeing what's hidden, and the warm yellow UI constantly evokes clarity and warmth. The CRT scan line overlay on battle screens integrates medium critique into moment-to-moment play. Persona 4 is arguably the most thematically coherent UI system on the PS2 — and it did it with Arial Bold and Baskerville.
