# Ico — PS2 UI/HUD/Menu Aesthetics

## Title Screen

- **Look:** Ico's title screen is characteristically minimal. A single stylized rendering of the boy (Ico) with his distinctive horns, rendered in soft, painterly tones. The palette is muted — pale stone greys, bleached whites, dusty taupes — evoking the isolation of the castle setting.
- **Type treatment:** The "ICO" logotype uses a compressed, sparse letterform. Community discussions on dafont.com trace the title font to something close to a condensed geometric sans or a custom/modified typeface — not a common retail font. The letters are set tightly with minimal embellishment, in white or very light grey against the pale background. The overall impression is of engraved stone lettering.
- **Color:** The title palette centers on **pale cream** (`~#f0ead8`), **stone grey** (`~#9a9488`), and **washed-out ivory** (`~#e8e0cc`). No vivid color. The atmosphere recalls bleached watercolor or an old photograph left in sunlight.

## Menu / Pause Menu Pattern

- **Philosophy:** "Design by subtraction" — Fumito Ueda's guiding principle. Every UI element that interfered with immersion was removed.
- **HUD:** **None.** Ico is one of the most radical examples of HUD elimination on PS2. There is no health bar, no stamina meter, no map, no inventory, no item prompts, no button tutorials. The game does not even show health points.
- **Pause menu:** Bare minimum — the pause state exists but is visually stripped of all ornamentation. Text is minimal, in a plain serifed or lightly decorated font, white against dark.
- **Save mechanic:** Save points are stone couches in the game world (diegetic, world-embedded, no screen overlay).
- **Yorda communication:** Ico calls Yorda by pressing a button; the game never tells you to press it — you discover it through play. No button prompts on screen.

## HUD Elements

Ico has effectively **zero HUD**:
- No health display
- No stamina / endurance meter
- No minimap
- No waypoints or objective markers
- No item or inventory display
- No XP, level, or skill indicators
- The only feedback is diegetic: Ico stumbles when low health; Yorda's shadow-beings dragging her communicates urgency.

## Font Character

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Title font | Custom/modified condensed — possibly near a geometric sans with hand-finishing |
| Weight | Light to Regular |
| Tracking | Tight — letters nearly touching |
| Style | Stone-engraving aesthetic; no decorative flourishes |
| In-game text | Minimal; subtitles in a plain thin sans for Yorda's alien language |

**Note on language:** Yorda speaks in a fictional language with untranslated subtitles (in the original Japanese/EU release). This was itself a design choice — unintelligibility reinforces otherness. The subtitle font is a thin, slightly archaic-looking serif.

## Color Palette / Grading

| Name | Approx Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Bleached Ivory | `#f0ead8` | Title screen base, castle stonework |
| Stone Grey | `#9a9488` | Mid-tone walls, shadow |
| Warm White | `#e8e0cc` | Title logotype |
| Deep Shadow | `#3a3228` | Darkest corners, near-black |
| Dusty Taupe | `#c8bfa8` | Ambient atmospheric tone |

**Overall grading:** The game world itself is rendered in desaturated warm-cool contrast — bright overexposed windows, pale stone corridors, warm sun shafts. Ueda's team used what was described as overexposed, desaturated light as a signature aesthetic. The palette is almost entirely achromatic with warm undertones. No saturated colors exist in the UI (because there is no UI to have them).

## Design Influence Notes

- Ico was critically described as "defined by what is not there." The subtraction of UI was not a technical constraint but a deliberate artistic choice to keep the central emotional mechanic (protecting Yorda) uninterrupted by game-layer abstraction.
- The game's visual inspiration cited by Ueda: Neoclassical etchings of Piranesi (prison architecture) and metaphysical paintings of Giorgio de Chirico (strange, depopulated spaces with long shadows).
- The soft, muted colors of the box art and title screen "convey a sense of calm, but more significantly, emphasize the profound loneliness that permeates the game."
